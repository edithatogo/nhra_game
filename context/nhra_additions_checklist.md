## Additional inputs worth modelling

| Add-on input | What to parameterise | Why it changes incentives |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Public health funding stream** | Treat “public health” as a third funding category alongside ABF + block (with its own rules/allocations) | Opens an additional strategic channel for shifting activity/spend across categories (and for Commonwealth–state bargaining) |
| **Data matching/business rules** | Matching rates, exclusion rules, revisions, dispute rates | Small rule changes can materially affect final NWAU, reconciliations, and “gaming” opportunities |
| **Payment system frictions** | Monthly payment cadence, reconciliation lags, cashflow constraints | Converts “entitlement” into operational behaviour (timing games, end-of-year surges) |
| **Schedule K one-off adjustments** | 2025–26 one-time fixed top-up; NT uplift to contribution rate; any earmarks | Large exogenous transfers reshape the bargaining set and effort incentives in that year |
| **Closing the Gap / equity commitments as utility terms** | Penalties/bonuses (political or reputational) for equity performance | Makes equity outcomes strategic rather than “nice to have” in the payoff function |
| **Mid-term review / evaluation pressure** | Probability of adverse review; recommendation adoption likelihood | Adds an “anticipated scrutiny” dynamic (actors pre-emptively adjust behaviour) |
| **Policy uncertainty scenarios** | Alternate futures (cap removed, contribution rate changes, split funding rules) | Lets you test robustness under realistic political proposals / reforms |

## Additional mechanisms to include

| Add-on mechanism | How to model it (game form) | What it captures |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Tri-stream funding choice (ABF vs block vs public health)** | Players can allocate marginal effort/volume across streams with different marginal returns | Strategic “classification/venue” shifting rather than only volume growth |
| **Administrator/NHFB as an independent rule-executor with integrity functions** | Mechanism designer / referee: calculates, pays, reconciles; integrity checks affect expected payoffs | Makes enforcement/processing a real strategic constraint, not an afterthought |
| **End-of-month processing + annual reconciliation loop** | Repeated game with short-cycle cashflow + long-cycle true-up | Timing strategies: “bring forward activity”, smoothing, defensive reporting |
| **Eligibility determination for block funded services/functions** | Leader move (IHACPA) then responses by states/providers | A key boundary-setting decision that creates substitution incentives |
| **Price-setting as Stackelberg leadership** | IHACPA sets NEP/weights; states respond with mix/volume/coding | Lets you model anticipation and lobbying/signalling for future determinations |
| **Cap + redistribution as a contest / common-pool problem** | Payoff kink at the cap; redistribution rule creates inter-state strategic interaction | “Race to cap”, timing games, and strategic restraint depending on expectations |
| **One-off side-payments (Schedule K) as bargaining settlement** | Transfer as equilibrium outcome in a bargaining stage (or exogenous shock) | Models “keep negotiations alive” dynamics and political settlement behaviour |

## Additional outputs that make the model diagnostically useful

| Add-on output | What to report | What it helps you conclude |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Stream-shifting indicators** | ABF share vs block vs public health over time; “boundary switching” | Whether reforms change *where* activity is recorded/funded, not just how much |
| **Reconciliation volatility** | Size/frequency of true-ups; revision rates; lag to finality | Whether the system is stable or produces chronic “surprise” adjustments |
| **Integrity/audit yield metrics** | Expected improper payment, detection rate, repayments/penalties | Quantifies the equilibrium level of gaming under different enforcement regimes |
| **Cashflow stress** | Months with negative net position; reliance on state advances; timing of deposits/payments | Links rules to operational stress and real-world managerial behaviour |
| **Equity/Closing the Gap performance** | Differential access/outcomes by priority group/region; equity-weighted welfare | Shows whether strategic behaviour is equity-improving or equity-eroding |
| **Reform robustness under policy counterfactuals** | Outcomes under “cap removed” or contribution-rate changes | Makes the simulation useful for policy design and negotiation prep |
