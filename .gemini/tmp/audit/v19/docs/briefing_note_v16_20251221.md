# Briefing note (v16): NHRA negotiations as governance-and-safety problem

**Date:** 21 December 2025  
**Audience:** RACMA Board/Council; senior officials and ministerial advisers  

## The problem
Hospitals are experiencing persistent throughput constraints driven by discharge failures and fragmented upstream capacity. In a split Commonwealth–State system, constrained Commonwealth-funded services (primary care access, aged care placement capacity, disability supports) shift demand and length of stay into State-funded acute services. This drives ED crowding and ambulance offload delay — a direct clinical safety risk.

## Why funding-only changes are unlikely to be sufficient
A headline Commonwealth share target (e.g., “45%”) is sensitive to the valuation base (the **National Efficient Price**, an annual $/NWAU applied to activity weights) and does not, on its own, realign incentives. Without structural alignment, additional funds can be absorbed by the same fragmentation and discharge bottlenecks that currently create access block.

## What RACMA should ask for (mechanism-linked)
1. **Indexation realism**: ensure valuation/indexation reflects workforce cost growth and rural cost structure, or explicitly address the “efficiency gap” in regional services.
2. **Integration conditions for new models**: Commonwealth-funded Urgent Care Clinics should be contractually integrated with Local Health Network clinical governance, information continuity, and escalation pathways.
3. **Regional pooled pilots**: pilot pooled regional funding arrangements that unify incentives across GP, hospital, and aged care interfaces, with agreed safety/accountability metrics.
4. **Discharge pathway capacity**: make discharge coordination and aged care placement capacity explicit levers within the reform package, tied to measurable reductions in long-stay occupancy.

## How to use the model outputs
Treat the modelling as a **mechanism map** and scenario comparator. The model supports the claim that governance/integration interventions are high leverage for throughput safety proxies, and that “funding-only” adjustments are less robust under uncertainty.
