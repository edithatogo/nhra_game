# Simulated peer review — Round 3 (v18) — 2025-12-21


## Overall assessment
The manuscript has improved by introducing explicit NEP ($/NWAU) and cost drift, which better reflects the reality that valuation alignment deteriorates when input costs outpace indexation. The system dynamics still rely on stylised proxies, but the directionality is clinically plausible (exit block → delays → risk).

## Major comments
1. **Clinical endpoint validity**: The “RR proxy” remains an abstract construct. If the paper is aimed at MJA readers, it should be more clearly anchored to recognisable outcomes (e.g., time-to-treatment, adverse events, mortality proxies, left-without-being-seen, ICU admissions). Even if you keep a proxy, it should be described as a *composite* and justified.
2. **Mechanism mapping**: The pathway from discharge delay and offload to safety should be described in a simple, clinician-friendly causal chain in the main text, with a figure linking “congestion” to “clinical deterioration”.
3. **Calibration transparency**: The NEP/cost drift parameters should be presented as explicit, plausible ranges (with sources later) and the analysis should emphasise that the absolute magnitudes are illustrative.

## Minor comments
- Provide a brief box explaining NEP × NWAU logic for readers unfamiliar with ABF.
- Add a short clinical vignette (1–2 sentences) to make the mechanism tangible.

## Recommendation
**Major revision**, but improved. The paper is getting closer to something clinically legible.
