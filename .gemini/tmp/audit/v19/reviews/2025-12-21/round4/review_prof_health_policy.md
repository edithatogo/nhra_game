# Round 4 peer-review simulation (v19)

Date: 2025-12-21

## Reviewer: Professor of Health Policy

### Overall recommendation
**Accept with minor revision.** The macro drift framing plus equilibrium mapping reads like a publishable policy methods piece.

### Major points
1. **Correct emphasis on equilibrium selection.** Enumerating equilibria and showing multiple equilibria under plausible parameterisations is the core policy insight: small governance commitments can flip the regime.
2. **NEP/cost drift as a political-economy driver.** The model’s “macro drift” module usefully illustrates why interface reform can matter as much as headline funding percentages.
3. **Implementation levers.** The intervention packages align with realistic levers: pooled funding/commissioning, UCC integration, and aged care/NDIS discharge capacity.

### Minor points
- Add a short “limitations” subsection explicitly stating what is omitted (e.g., detailed ABF classification, behavioural heterogeneity by state, provider market structure).
- Consider adding a sentence on how the framework could be used in deliberative policy design (e.g., identifying “minimum viable commitments” to select cooperative equilibria).

### Suggested edits
- In the scenario section, include one sentence about the practical governance instruments that correspond to each lever (e.g., pooled pilots, shared KPIs, enforceable interface standards).
