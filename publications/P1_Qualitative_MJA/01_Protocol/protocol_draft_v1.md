# Protocol: Structural Analysis of the National Health Reform Agreement using Game Theory (Draft v1.0)

## 1. Background & Rationale
The National Health Reform Agreement (NHRA), signed in 2011, fundamentally shifted Australian public hospital funding from block grants to Activity Based Funding (ABF). While intended to drive efficiency and transparency (Duckett, 2016), anecdotal evidence and theoretical critiques suggest the system may incentivize "gaming"—strategic behaviors where health services optimize for metric reporting rather than genuine patient safety ("Safety Theatre"). Despite this, no formal study has mapped the legal text of the NHRA to a rigorous game-theoretic framework to identify these structural vulnerabilities systematically.

## 2. Objectives
1.  **Map** the static game structure defined by the NHRA legislation and Addendums, identifying Players (Federal/State/LHNs), Action Sets (Funding/Reporting/Auditing), and Payoff Functions (NEP/HAC penalties).
2.  **Identify** structural incoherence or "undefined states" in the Agreement that permit non-cooperative equilibria (e.g., cost-shifting).
3.  **Determine** if the current incentive structures mathematically favor "Safety Theatre" (reporting compliance) over "High Reliability" (actual safety).

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 Study Design
This is a qualitative document analysis study using a directed graph approach to formalize legal text into game-theoretic nodes.

### 3.2 Information Sources
*   Primary: National Health Reform Agreement (2011) and subsequent Addendums (2017, 2020-2025).
*   Secondary: Determination reports from the Independent Health and Aged Care Pricing Authority (IHACPA).

### 3.3 Data Collection (Blinded Mapping)
Two independent researchers (simulated personas) will code the text using a "Clean Room" protocol.
*   **Coder A (Methodologist):** Will code strictly based on the text's explicit rules.
*   **Coder B (Policy Expert):** Will code based on applied policy interpretation.
They will extract:
*   *Players:* Who makes decisions?
*   *Moves:* What legal actions are available?
*   *Information Sets:* What do players know when they move?
*   *Payoffs:* What financial/political utility is derived?

### 3.4 Analysis & Synthesis
The coded extracts will be converted into directed graphs (Game Trees).
*   **Reconciliation:** Discrepancies between Coder A and B will be resolved by a third arbiter (Legal Scholar).
*   **Validation:** The final graph will be tested for logical completeness (do all paths end in a terminal node?).

## 4. Reporting
This study will be reported in accordance with the SRQR (Standards for Reporting Qualitative Research) checklist.
