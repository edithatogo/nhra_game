# Protocol: Structural Analysis of the National Health Reform Agreement using Game Theory (Draft v1.1)

## 1. Background & Rationale
The National Health Reform Agreement (NHRA), signed in 2011, fundamentally shifted Australian public hospital funding from block grants to Activity Based Funding (ABF). While intended to drive efficiency and transparency (Duckett, 2016), anecdotal evidence and theoretical critiques suggest the system may incentivize "gaming"—strategic behaviors where health services optimize for metric reporting rather than genuine patient safety ("Safety Theatre"). Despite this, no formal study has mapped the legal text of the NHRA to a rigorous game-theoretic framework to identify these structural vulnerabilities systematically.

## 2. Objectives
1.  **Map** the static game structure defined by the NHRA legislation and Addendums, identifying Players (Federal/State/LHNs), Action Sets (Funding/Reporting/Auditing), and Payoff Functions (NEP/HAC penalties).
2.  **Analyze** potential structural incoherence or "undefined states" in the Agreement, contrasting these with intentional "Constructive Ambiguity" often found in inter-governmental policy.
3.  **Determine** if the current incentive structures mathematically favor "Safety Theatre" (reporting compliance) over "High Reliability" (actual safety) using Extensive Form Game analysis.

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 Study Design
This is a qualitative document analysis study using an Extensive Form Game with Imperfect Information approach to formalize legal and policy text into game-theoretic nodes.

### 3.2 Information Sources
*   **Primary:** National Health Reform Agreement (2011), subsequent Addendums (2017, 2020-2025), and the *National Health Reform Act 2011* (Cth).
*   **Secondary:** Determination reports and Pricing Frameworks from the Independent Health and Aged Care Pricing Authority (IHACPA).

### 3.3 Data Collection (Blinded Mapping)
Two independent researchers (simulated personas) will code the text using a "Clean Room" protocol.
*   **Coder A (Methodologist):** Will code strictly based on the text's explicit rules and legal requirements.
*   **Coder B (Policy Expert):** Will code based on applied policy interpretation and historical operational context.
They will extract:
*   *Players:* Decision-making entities (Federal Minister, State Health Ministers, LHN Boards).
*   *Moves:* Legally available actions (e.g., Data Submission, Funding Determination, Audit Initiation).
*   *Information Sets:* Knowledge available to players at the time of each move (e.g., contemporaneous vs. lagged data).
*   *Payoffs:* Financial (NWAU payments) and political (Compliance/KPI achievement) utility.

### 3.4 Analysis & Synthesis
The coded extracts will be converted into directed graphs representing Extensive Form Games.
*   **Reconciliation:** Discrepancies between Coder A and B will be resolved by a third arbiter (Legal Scholar).
*   **Validation:** The final graph will be tested for logical completeness and checked for non-cooperative equilibria that emerge from "Constructive Ambiguity."

### 3.5 Reflexivity & Bias (Simulated)
This study utilizes Large Language Model (LLM) agents to simulate expert personas. We acknowledge the inherent bias toward "textbook" interpretations of policy and the risk of hallucinating legal nuance. To mitigate this, multiple personas with divergent professional priorities (Methodologist vs. Policy Expert) are used to simulate inter-rater reliability checks.

## 4. Reporting
This study will be reported in accordance with the SRQR (Standards for Reporting Qualitative Research) checklist.
