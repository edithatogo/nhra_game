# RACMA Position Statement: Structural Reform of the National Health Reform Agreement (NHRA)

**Author:** Dylan A Mordaunt

## Executive Summary / Key Position

The Royal Australasian College of Medical Administrators (RACMA) asserts that the current structural architecture of the National Health Reform Agreement (NHRA) requires fundamental reform to align financial incentives with high-reliability clinical outcomes. Our research indicates that the existing framework of Activity Based Funding (ABF), characterized by significant information asymmetries and reporting lags, has unintentionally created a "Strategic Gaming" equilibrium. In this state, healthcare organizations are incentivized to optimize for symbolic metric compliance and revenue maximization rather than substantive patient safety and system performance improvements. RACMA calls for an urgent transition from punitive, lag-driven audit regimes toward a "Transparency Surge" model that prioritizes contemporaneous, high-quality performance signals and the neutralization of statutory constructive ambiguity.

## Background / Context

Since the inception of the NHRA in 2011, the Australian public hospital system has undergone a massive shift toward Activity Based Funding (ABF) {Council on Federal Financial Relations, 2011 @NHRA_2011 #106}. While the primary goal was to drive efficiency through the National Efficient Price (NEP), the system's reliance on lagged data and complex growth caps has introduced significant strategic vulnerabilities.

Recent qualitative mapping and quantitative simulation studies (Mordaunt, 2025) have identified "Strategic Gaming" as a rational response to these vulnerabilities. This behavior, grounded in the sociological construct of **Institutional Isomorphism**, forces Local Health Networks (LHNs) to adopt symbolic "performance masks" to maintain legitimacy and financial stability in a high-stakes regulatory environment {DiMaggio & Powell, 1983 @DiMaggio_1983 #110}. The current reliance on Patient Safety Indicator 90 (PSI-90) and Hospital Acquired Complication (HAC) adjustments provides noisy signals that often decouple reported metrics from genuine clinical effort.

## Key Issues

1. **Vertical Fiscal Imbalance (VFI) and Information Asymmetry:** The distance between the Commonwealth (Principal) and the clinical coalface (LHNs) creates wide information sets that strategic agents can exploit to prioritize revenue under the 6.5% cap.
2. **Information Lags and Moral Hazard:** The 12-24 month delay between service delivery and audit reconciliation allows agents to prioritize immediate financial survival over long-term clinical quality.
3. **Constructive Ambiguity in Statutory Text:** Intentional vagueness in the NHRA and its Addendums creates "undefined states" in the incentive grammar, facilitating cost-shifting and reporting manipulation.
4. **Punitive vs. Productive Regulation:** Current audit-blitz models increase administrative burden without corresponding gains in clinical throughput or safety {Duckett, 2021 @Duckett_2021 #105}.

## Recommendations / Call to Action

RACMA recommends the following structural reforms to the NHRA negotiation framework:

* **Prioritize Transparency Surges:** Transition from retrospective audits to the contemporaneous publication of granular, validated performance data to narrow the information gap.
* **Reduce Indexation Divergence:** Align the NEP and growth caps with actual health sector input costs (WPI) to eliminate the financial necessity of Strategic Gaming.
* **Neutralize Constructive Ambiguity:** Clarify statutory language regarding "substitute services" and "data quality loops" to eliminate cost-shifting incentives.
* **Strengthen Clinical Governance:** Shift the focus of accountability from documentation intensity to High Reliability Organizational (HRO) principles.
