# The "Hybrid Model": Why we can't explain the health crisis with one chart

You often hear simple explanations for the hospital crisis.
"It's the NDIS."
"It's GP shortages."
"It's bureaucracy."

They are all right. And all wrong.
Because the health system isn't a single game. It's a **Network of Games**.

## The real-world situation

The **NHRA Game** is an ensemble of interacting pressures.
- **Upstream**: Cost shifting affects ED demand.
- **Midstream**: Internal competition affects hospital budgets.
- **Downstream**: Discharge coordination affects bed block.
- **Overlay**: Electoral cycles affect funding stability.

## The "game" incentives

We model this as a **Hybrid Ensemble**.
We take the "micro-games" (like Bed Block or Upcoding) and stack them together.

If the Commonwealth cuts GP funding (Stackelberg Game), it triggers an ED surge (Queuing Game), which worsens bed block (Discharge Game), which forces the State to cut elective surgery (Internal Competition Game).

## The Equilibrium: "Systemic Drag"

When you stack these frictions, you get **Systemic Drag**.
Small inefficiencies in one layer multiply as they move through the system.
A 5% drop in GP access can cause a 20% spike in ambulance ramping.

## How to fix it

You can't fix a complex system with a simple lever.
You need **System-Level Reform**.

- **Pooled Funding**: Removes the cost-shifting incentive.
- **Integrated Governance**: Removes the principal-agent friction.
- **Long-term Agreements**: Removes the electoral boom-bust.
