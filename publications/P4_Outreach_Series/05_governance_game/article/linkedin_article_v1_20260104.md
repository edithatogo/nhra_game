# The "Governance Game": why health reform feels like a tug-of-war

Is it better to control everything from the center, or let local hospitals run themselves?

This is the **Governance Game**. And it's not just about management styles—it's about incentives.

In the NHRA context, we see a constant tension between **Local Hospital Networks (LHNs)** and **State Ministries of Health (MOH)**.

## The real-world situation

- **The Ministry (MOH)** wants control, consistency, and standardisation.
- **The Hospital (LHN)** wants autonomy, agility, and the ability to fix local problems fast.

## The "game" incentives

We model this as a Principal-Agent problem.

- **Integrate (Control)**: The MOH centralizes decision-making.
- **Separate (Delegate)**: The MOH gives the LHN autonomy.

If the MOH controls too tightly, the LHN loses "agency"—they stop innovating and just follow orders.
If the MOH lets go too much, they risk "fragmentation"—every hospital doing its own thing, making the system unmanageable.

## The Equilibrium: "Separate" (but with friction)

Often, the Nash equilibrium is **Separate**.
LHNs fight hard for autonomy. Ministries realize that micro-managing 100 hospitals is impossible.

But without clear rules, this separation creates friction.
"Why didn't you tell us about that deficit?"
"Why did you impose that new policy without asking?"

## How to fix it

The goal isn't total control OR total freedom. It's **Earned Autonomy**.

- **Clear boundaries**: Define exactly what is local vs central.
- **Data transparency**: If the Ministry can see the data in real-time, they don't need to control the process.
- **Trust**: Earned through performance, not just demanded.

## TL;DR

- The "Governance Game" is a tug-of-war between Central Control and Local Autonomy.
- Both sides have valid incentives.
- The solution is "Earned Autonomy" backed by real-time data transparency.
