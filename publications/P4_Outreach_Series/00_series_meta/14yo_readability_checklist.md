# 14yo Readability Checklist

Use this checklist before calling a draft “ready”.

## Language
- Define acronyms the first time (one short line).
- Prefer everyday words (e.g., “deal” instead of “agreement”, “rules” instead of “framework”) unless precision matters.
- Avoid long noun-stacks (“activity-based funding parameter calibration”) — break them up.

## Structure
- Start with the NHRA story before introducing game theory.
- Include one concrete mini-example (“imagine X… then Y happens”).
- Add a short “What this is NOT” section if the topic could sound accusatory.

## Sentences
- Keep most sentences short.
- Prefer active voice.
- Use bullet points for lists of incentives, players, or policy levers.

## Concepts
- Explain “equilibrium” as “what tends to stick” (not “best”).
- Explain “incentives” as “what gets rewarded or punished”.
- When using a model term, give a one-line intuition (no math required).

## Humour and tone
- Light humour is fine, but never at the expense of patients or staff.
- Avoid blame framing; focus on system incentives and measurement.

## Visuals
- Every diagram needs: title + 1-line caption + alt text.
- Ensure diagrams are readable on a phone (large labels, low clutter).
