## Panel feedback

### 1) “14yo reader” (curious, zero policy background)

- Scores: Clarity 4, Accuracy 4, Humour 2, 14yo 4, Policy 3
- What worked: The “agree now vs hold out” choice is easy; “time isn’t free” makes sense.
- Confusing: “discounting” still feels like jargon; wants a real-life analogy (pizza / chores / phone battery).
- Suggestion: Add one tiny concrete example of what “hold out” looks like (without getting legalistic).

### 2) State health department negotiator (pragmatic, system incentives lens)

- Scores: Clarity 4, Accuracy 4, Humour 2, 14yo 4, Policy 4
- What worked: Policy levers are sensible and non-partisan; avoids blaming.
- Risk: Could imply negotiations are “adversarial” only; in reality there are repeated interactions + consultation constraints.
- Suggestion: Add a short “What this is NOT” section: not accusing actors; not claiming a single one-shot game explains everything.

### 3) Commonwealth finance / accountability perspective

- Scores: Clarity 4, Accuracy 4, Humour 2, 14yo 4, Policy 4
- What worked: Clear link between incentives and delay; “default outcomes” framing is useful.
- Suggestion: Emphasise that transparency and measurable rules can reduce bargaining space and reduce hold-outs.

### 4) Hospital manager / clinician operations (frontline planning)

- Scores: Clarity 4, Accuracy 4, Humour 2, 14yo 4, Policy 4
- What worked: “Uncertainty costs” resonates (workforce planning, capital, bed flow).
- Suggestion: Add 1–2 bullets about where uncertainty hurts (recruitment, service planning) and keep it human (patients).

### 5) Journalist/editor (story + hook)

- Scores: Clarity 4, Accuracy 4, Humour 3, 14yo 4, Policy 3
- What worked: Strong opening; clean headings; no math.
- Suggestion: Make the LinkedIn post hook punchier (question format). Add one light joke/analogy (not flippant).

### 6) Game theory academic (keep it correct but simple)

- Scores: Clarity 4, Accuracy 4, Humour 2, 14yo 4, Policy 3
- Note: The matrix looks like a “chicken / war-of-attrition flavour” situation; equilibrium can be multiple or mixed.
- Suggestion: Add one sentence: “Depending on payoffs, there may be more than one equilibrium; the point is the incentive to wait.”

## Cross-cutting improvements (top themes)

- Add a tiny concrete example (what “hold out” might look like) without claiming specifics.
- Add an analogy for “discounting” that a teenager would actually use.
- Add a short “What this is NOT” disclaimer to avoid sounding accusatory or too literal.
- Improve the LinkedIn post hook and include a small professional joke.
