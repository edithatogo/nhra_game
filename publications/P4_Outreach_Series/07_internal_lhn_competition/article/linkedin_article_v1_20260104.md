# Internal Competition: Why hospitals fight over "profitable" patients

We like to think of the public health system as one big happy family.
But inside, different Local Hospital Networks (LHNs) are often competing.

They compete for staff, for prestige, and most importantly—for **Activity-Based Funding (ABF)** revenue.

## The real-world situation

Under ABF, some services are "profitable" (the price > cost) and some are "loss-making" (cost > price).

- **LHN A (Urban)** has scale and can pick "winners" (e.g., high-volume elective surgery).
- **LHN B (Regional)** has to take everyone, including complex, low-margin cases.

## The "game" incentives

We model this as a Revenue Capture game.

- **Revenue Capture**: Focus on high-margin services to build a surplus.
- **Service Pressure**: Take the difficult cases that drain the budget.

If LHN A captures the revenue, they can hire better staff and buy better equipment.
LHN B is left with the "Service Pressure" and a deficit.

## The Equilibrium: "Urban Dominance"

The Nash equilibrium is for the powerful LHN to aggressively capture revenue.
The regional LHN is forced into a deficit spiral.

This isn't because the regional managers are bad. It's because the *price signal* incentivizes cherry-picking.

## How to fix it

- **Block funding for safety nets**: Don't use ABF for essential but unprofitable services (like rural EDs).
- **Network balancing**: Force LHNs to share the load of complex cases, not just dump them on the "sink" hospital.
- **Risk adjustment**: Adjust the price signal to account for complexity so "cherry-picking" doesn't pay.

## Evidence / further reading

- Productivity Commission Report on Efficiency 2022.
- Independent Hospital Pricing Authority (IHACPA) Technical Specs.

## TL;DR

- Hospitals compete for "profitable" patients under ABF.
- This hurts regional/safety-net hospitals who get left with the complex cases.
- We need to adjust the price signals to stop cherry-picking.
