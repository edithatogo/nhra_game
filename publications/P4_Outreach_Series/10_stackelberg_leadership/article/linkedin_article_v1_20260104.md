# Stackelberg Leadership: The power of moving first

In negotiations, is it better to move first or second?

In Game Theory, the **Stackelberg Leadership** model shows that if you can credibly commit to a move first, you can force the other player to react to *your* terms.

## The real-world situation

The Commonwealth often acts as the "Stackelberg Leader" in health funding.
It sets the Medicare rebate or the Aged Care funding envelope unilaterally.

The States ("Followers") have to optimize their hospital budgets *given* the Commonwealth's choice.

## The "game" incentives

- **Leader (Commonwealth)**: Chooses a funding level ($L$) to maximize their own utility, knowing how the State will react.
- **Follower (State)**: Observes $L$ and chooses their own funding ($F$) to do the best they can.

Because the Leader *anticipates* the Follower's reaction function, they can manipulate the outcome.
"If I cut GP funding, the State *will* have to increase ED funding. So I can cut safely."

## The Equilibrium: "First-Mover Advantage"

The Leader captures more surplus than in a simultaneous (Cournot) game.
They effectively "force" the Follower to pick up the slack.

But this relies on **commitment**. If the Leader can't stick to their guns (e.g., voters revolt), the advantage collapses.

## How to fix it

- **Joint decision making**: Remove the Leader/Follower dynamic. Make decisions simultaneous or consensus-based (like the National Cabinet).
- **Credible threats**: States need to show they *won't* automatically pick up the slack (e.g., by capping ED capacity), forcing the Leader to reconsider.

## Evidence / further reading

- Stackelberg, H. (1934). *Market Structure and Equilibrium*.
- Productivity Commission (2023).

## TL;DR

- Moving first allows you to manipulate the other player's choice.
- The Commonwealth often uses "First-Mover Advantage" to shift costs.
- States need to change the game from Leader-Follower to Joint-Decision.
