1/ Is it better to move first or second in a negotiation? ♟️

In health funding, the Commonwealth moves first.
And that gives them **Stackelberg Leadership**. 🧵

2/ The Setup:
Leader (Commonwealth) sets a policy (e.g., GP Rebates). 📢
Follower (State) sees the policy, then sets their own (e.g., Hospital Budgets). 🏥

3/ The Logic:
The Leader knows the Follower is rational.
"If I underfund Primary Care, the State *must* fund the ED overflow to avoid a crisis."

So the Leader can "bank" the saving, knowing the State will fill the gap. 🏦

4/ This is the **First-Mover Advantage.**
You define the constraints the other player has to live in.

It explains why cost-shifting often flows DOWNHILL (Federal -> State). ⬇️

5/ The Equilibrium:
The Leader captures the surplus.
The Follower picks up the tab (and the risk).

This persists as long as the Leader's commitment is "credible." (i.e., they won't panic and U-turn).

6/ How to break it?
States need to stop being predictable Followers.
"If you cut GP funding, we WON'T expand the ED. We'll send them back to you."

(High risk, but it changes the Leader's calculus). 🛑

7/ Ideally, we move to **Joint Decision Making**.
National Cabinet is an attempt to turn Stackelberg (Leader/Follower) into Cooperative Nash (Partners). 🤝
