1/ Why does the person in a rush always lose the negotiation? 🏃‍♂️💸

It’s the **Rubinstein Bargaining** effect.

Here is how it shapes the National Health Reform Agreement (NHRA). 🧵

2/ The Setup:
Two players (Federal & State) splitting a pie (Funding).
They take turns making offers.
Offer 1... Reject. Offer 2... Reject.

But TIME has a cost.
Every day you delay, the pie shrinks (or you suffer). 🥧➡️🍪

3/ The concept: **Discount Factors.**
If I am "Patient" (Low discount), I don't mind waiting.
If I am "Impatient" (High discount), I need the deal NOW.

4/ The Equilibrium:
We don't actually wait forever.
The Patient player knows the Impatient player is desperate.
So they make an offer on Day 1 that extracts almost all the surplus.

The Impatient player says "Yes" because a bad deal today is better than a slightly better deal next year. 🤝

5/ In Health:
States run the hospitals. They feel the pain of a funding delay immediately. (High Impatience).
The Commonwealth writes the cheques. They can often wait. (High Patience).

Result? The deal tilts to the Commonwealth. 🏛️

6/ The Lesson:
Power isn't just about size. It's about your **BATNA** (Best Alternative to a Negotiated Agreement) and your **Patience**.

If States want better NHRA terms, they need the fiscal capacity to walk away and wait. 🛑
