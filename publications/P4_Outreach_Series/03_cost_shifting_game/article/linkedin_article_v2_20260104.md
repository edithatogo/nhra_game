# The “Cost Shifting Game”: why split funding can make us move costs instead of fixing problems

Why does it sometimes feel like the health system is just moving a problem from one bucket to another?

In Australia, the answer is often found in the **National Health Reform Agreement (NHRA)**.

We have a split system:
- **Commonwealth** pays for GPs and aged care.
- **States** pay for public hospitals.

This split creates a **Cost Shifting Game**.

## The real-world situation

Imagine a patient needs care.

1.  **If GP access is hard:** The patient goes to the ED. The Commonwealth saves a Medicare rebate; the State pays for an expensive hospital visit.
2.  **If hospital discharge is rushed:** The patient leaves early but isn't ready. They end up back at the GP or needing Federal aged care. The State saves a bed-day; the Commonwealth pays for the fix.

## The “game” incentives

We model this as a choice between two strategies:

- **Invest**: Spend money now to prevent a problem later.
- **Shift**: Save your own budget and let the other side handle the fallout.

In a perfect world, everyone invests. But in the "game" of split budgets, **Shift** is often the strategic "best response."

Why? Because if you Invest and the other side Shifts, you lose twice: you pay for your improvement *and* you inherit their mess.

## The result: "System Failure"

Economists call this an **externality** (making someone else pay for your choice).

When both sides shift costs, we get a "System Failure" outcome.
- Budgets look "efficient" on paper.
- Patients get stuck in the gaps (waiting lists, ambulance ramping, bed block).
- The total cost to the taxpayer actually goes *up*.

## How to fix it

You can't just ask people to "be nicer." You have to change the rules of the game.

- **Pooled funding**: If the money comes from one bucket, shifting costs doesn't save anyone anything.
- **Patient-centred measurement**: Measure the entire journey, not just "activity" in one building.
- **Transparency**: Use data to show exactly where the costs are moving.

## Evidence / further reading

- Senate Inquiry into Hospital Funding (2016). *Final Report*.

## TL;DR

- Split funding incentives encourage cost shifting.
- It is often "rational" for budgets to shift costs, even if it hurts patients.
- To fix it, we need incentives that reward fixing the problem, not moving it.
