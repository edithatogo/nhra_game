1/ Ever feel like the health system is just passing the buck? 💸🏥

In Australia, the "buck" moves between the Commonwealth and the States.

We call this the **NHRA Cost Shifting Game**.

Here is why it happens (and why it's not just politicians being mean). 🧵

2/ The setup:
🔹 Commonwealth pays for GPs & Aged Care.
🔹 States pay for Hospitals.

This split creates a "Game." ♟️

If I save money in my bucket, it often creates a cost in yours.

3/ Scenario A:
GP access gets harder (Federal saving).
⬇️
Patients go to ED (State cost). 🚑

Scenario B:
Hospital discharges early (State saving).
⬇️
Patient needs intense GP/Aged Care support (Federal cost). 🔁

4/ In Game Theory, we look at "Invest" vs "Shift."

Ideally, both sides **Invest** to fix the root cause.
But the incentive is to **Shift**—save your budget and let the other side pay.

If you Invest and they Shift, you pay double. So you Shift too. 🛡️

5/ The Result: "System Failure."

Both sides save money on paper.
But the patient gets stuck in the middle.
And the TOTAL cost to the taxpayer goes UP. 📈

6/ Economists call this a "Negative Externality."
I make a choice, you pay the price.

The NHRA tries to fix this with activity-based funding, but the split-budget incentive is strong. 💪

7/ The Fix?
You have to change the game.

✅ Pooled Funding (One bucket = no shifting).
✅ Patient Outcomes (Measure the journey).
✅ Transparency (Track the cost flow).

8/ We're modelling these dynamics to see which policy levers work best.
